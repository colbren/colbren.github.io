Project 0: Becoming Friends with Your Camera

Part 1: Selfie: The Wrong Way vs. The Right Way

The close-up was shot from just a foot or two away, and it distorts my face making my nose looks enlarged, while the ears and the outline of the head shrink back. At close range, the nose is a much bigger fraction of the total distance to the lens than the ears are, so it subtends a wider angle and appears magnified relative to the rest of the face. In the second shot, I stepped back several feet and zoomed in to match the face's size in frame. Now that the I am farther away, the distance between the nose and the ears is small relative to the total distance, so every part of the face is rendered at roughly the same scale resulting in a look that is far more natural.

Part 2: Architectural Perspective Compression

Same effect as Part 1, but on a building instead of a face. In the first photo, I stood far away and zoomed in until the building looked about the same size as in the second photo. At that distance, the near and far edges of the facade are almost equally far from the camera, so the depth flattens. In the second photo, taken up close with no zoom, the near corner is much closer to the camera than the far corner. That difference in distance is now huge relative to the total distance, so you get strong perspective. Zooming in from far away compresses perspective while getting physically close exaggerates it.
